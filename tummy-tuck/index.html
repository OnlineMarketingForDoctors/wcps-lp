What a tummy tuck actually does

Abdominoplasty works on two layers at once. It repairs the muscle wall that holds your stomach in, then removes the stretched skin and fat sitting over it.

  1. A low incision

    Made from hip to hip, low enough to sit beneath most underwear and swimwear.

  2. Muscle repair

    The separated rectus muscles are stitched back together, tightening your core like an internal corset.

  3. Skin and fat removed

    Loose skin is lifted, the excess is removed and liposuction can refine the waist and flanks.

  4. A new belly button

    Your own navel is brought through the tightened skin in a natural position, and the wound is closed in layers.

Is a tummy tuck right for you?

You may be a good candidate if you

  • Have loose skin or a separated muscle wall that exercise has not changed
  • Are at, or close to, a weight you have kept stable for 6 to 12 months
  • Have finished having children
  • Are a non-smoker, or can stop for at least 6 weeks either side of surgery
  • Are in good general health with realistic expectations

It may be better to wait if you

  • Are still losing weight or plan to lose a lot more
  • Are planning a future pregnancy
  • Smoke or vape and cannot stop
  • Have uncontrolled diabetes, heart or lung disease, or a history of blood clots
  • Are hoping surgery will replace weight loss

Your safety comes first. Dr Khan reviews your full medical history, may ask for tests or a GP referral, and in line with Australian guidelines you will have two consultations and at least seven days to reflect before booking surgery.

Frequently asked questions

Is tummy tuck surgery safe?

Abdominoplasty is a well-established operation when performed by a Specialist Plastic Surgeon in an accredited hospital. Like all surgery it carries risks, which Dr Khan explains in detail. Careful assessment, stopping smoking and following your aftercare plan all lower your risk.

How painful is recovery?

Most people describe tightness and soreness rather than sharp pain, especially after muscle repair. It is strongest in the first few days and is controlled with prescribed medication. Walking slightly bent forward for the first week or so is normal.

How long will I need off work?

Most patients return to desk work after 2 to 3 weeks. Jobs involving lifting or physical effort usually need 6 weeks. Driving is generally possible once you can brake suddenly without discomfort, often around 2 to 3 weeks.

Will I have a scar?

Yes. Every tummy tuck leaves a scar, placed low so it can be hidden by most underwear and swimwear. It is pink at first and gradually softens and fades over 12 to 18 months. Scar care advice is part of your aftercare.

Does a tummy tuck remove fat?

Yes. Fat is removed along with the excess skin, and liposuction is often added to refine the waist and flanks. A tummy tuck is not a weight loss operation, though. It works best when you are already close to your goal weight.

Can I have children after a tummy tuck?

You can, but pregnancy will stretch the skin and muscles again and may undo much of the result. Most women are advised to wait until they have finished having children.

Is a tummy tuck covered by Medicare or private health?

A Medicare item number may apply when there is a documented medical need, for example significant muscle separation after pregnancy with symptoms, or excess skin after major weight loss causing recurrent rashes. If an item number applies, your private health fund may contribute to hospital costs. We will check your eligibility at consultation.

How long do the results last?

The muscle repair and skin removal are long lasting. Your result is best protected by keeping a stable weight and healthy lifestyle. Ageing, pregnancy and weight changes can still affect your shape over time.

Risks and complications

Every operation carries risk, and you deserve a clear picture before you decide. Dr Khan discusses each of these with you, explains how likely they are for you personally, and how we work to prevent them.

Any surgical or invasive procedure carries risks. Before proceeding, you should seek a second opinion from an appropriately qualified health practitioner.

  • Bleeding or haematoma
  • Infection
  • Seroma (fluid collection)
  • Delayed wound healing
  • Thickened or widened scars
  • Numbness of the lower abdomen
  • Belly button shape or position
  • Asymmetry or contour irregularity
  • Fat necrosis
  • Blood clots in the legs or lungs
  • Anaesthetic risks
  • Need for revision surgery
